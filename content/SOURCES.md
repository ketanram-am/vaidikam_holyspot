## Editorial rules applied

1. Deity names come from the ceremony name itself and are not interpreted.
2. Purpose tags state only what published reference works state. Where a
   tradition is contested, the page says less rather than picking a side.
3. No page states procedure, duration, materials, mantras, or whether a rite
   can be performed remotely. Those are consultation matters.
4. Nothing claims a guaranteed outcome.

## Samskaras

### Garbhadhana — "receiving the womb"
- First of the sixteen samskaras. Sanskrit *garbha* (womb) + *ādhāna*
  (receiving). A private rite marking a couple's intent to have a child,
  performed before conception. Grihya Sutra procedure has the wife dress
  formally while the husband recites Vedic verses. Textual roots in Rigveda
  8.35.10–12 and 10.184.
- <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garbhadhana> (cites P.V. Kane,
  *History of Dharmasastra*)

### Pumsavana — third or fourth month
- Second samskara, performed after the pregnancy is visible and around the
  time fetal movement begins. Roots in Atharva Veda 4.3.23, 4.6.2, 4.6.17.
- **Deliberate omission:** some texts attach a gender-selection element to
  this rite (banyan-leaf extract in one nostril or the other). The site does
  not mention it. The term *puṃsavana* is itself semantically contested —
  translations run from "quickening a male or female fetus" to "bringing
  forth a male baby" — so the page says only "quickening of the child",
  which every reading supports.
- <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pumsavana>

### Simantonnayana — "parting the hair upward"
- Third samskara, last trimester, usually the seventh or eighth month. The
  husband parts his wife's hair upward at least three times with udumbara
  fruit, porcupine quill, and Viratara wood, reciting *Bhūr, Bhuvaḥ, Svaḥ*.
  Stated purpose: healthy development of the child and safe delivery.
  Yajnavalkya Smriti stresses satisfying the mother's wishes to prevent
  miscarriage. Kane notes wide divergence in Grihyasutra detail.
- Regional names worth using with foreign families: Seemantham (South
  India), Valaikappu (Tamil), Godh-Bharai (Hindi belt).
- <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simantonnayana> (Paraskara Grihya Sutra,
  Baudhayana, Kane)

### The full sixteen (shodasha samskara)
- Order and timing below follow the Grihyasutra enumeration as summarised in
  Wikipedia's "Samskara (rite of passage)", which cites P.V. Kane and Raj Bali
  Pandey throughout.
- **Before birth:** Garbhadhana, Pumsavana (third month), Simantonnayana
  (eighth month).
- **Infancy:** Jatakarma (at birth), Namakarana (eleventh or twelfth day),
  Nishkramana (about the fourth month — first outing), Annaprashana (about the
  sixth month — first solid food), Chudakarana (near the first birthday —
  first tonsure), Karnavedha (ear piercing).
- **Learning:** Vidyarambha (traditionally the fifth year), Upanayana (sacred
  thread / beginning formal study), Vedarambha (beginning Vedic study).
- **Coming of age:** Keshanta (a young man's first shaving), Ritusuddhi (a
  young woman's coming of age), Samavartana (completion of study), Vivaha
  (marriage).
- **Antyeshti** — the final rites — is the sixteenth in the lists that include
  it. It is named on the page only as something arranged by conversation; a
  bereaved family should not meet a purpose tag.
- Counts genuinely vary: the Gautama Dharmasutra lists forty samskaras, and
  Grihyasutra texts enumerate between twelve and eighteen. "Sixteen" is the
  common later grouping, not the only canonical number, so the page says
  "the rites that mark a life" rather than claiming a fixed set.
- <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samskara_(rite_of_passage)>

## Homas

### Maha Sudarshana homa
- Addressed to Sudarshana, the discus of Vishnu, revered in Vaishnava
  tradition as a protective power of Vishnu himself and invoked for
  protection and cleansing. Associated with Narasimha, whose weapon it is.
- <https://ssvt.org/services/sri-sudarsana/> (Sri Siva Vishnu Temple)
- <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chakra_Narasimha_Temple>
- Weaker sources (commercial puja vendors) were found and **not** used.

### Vainateya homa
- *Vainateya* = "son of Vinatā", an epithet of Garuda. The name *garuḍa* is
  itself glossed as "one who consumes poison" or "one who removes poison"
  (*gara* + *ad* / *uḍ*). The Garuda Upanishad and the Garuḍamantra are
  specifically for alleviating poison and venom — snakebite and poison from
  other sources. Garuda is the vigilant protector and enemy of serpents.
- This is why the page says "against poison and venom" rather than a general
  "protection": the poison association is the documented, specific one.
- <https://www.wisdomlib.org/definition/vainateya>
- <https://www.wisdomlib.org/definition/garuda>
- <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garuda_Upanishad>

### Vayuputra homa
- *Vayuputra* = "son of Vāyu", an epithet of Hanuman.
- <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanuman>

### Vastu homa / Vastu rakshogña homa
- *Vastu shanti* is performed by a priest to propitiate the benevolent
  deities of the household and to ward off malignant influence; *grihapravesha*
  ("entrance into a house") is the rite on moving into a new home.
- *Rakṣoghna* (रक्षोघ्न) literally means "that which causes rakshasas to be
  driven off and destroyed". The **Rakṣoghna Sūkta** is prescribed for the
  founding of a new temple and in palace building — which is exactly why this
  homa attaches to a site rather than to a person.
- <https://www.wisdomlib.org/definition/rakshoghna>
- <https://www.wisdomlib.org/definition/rakshoghnasukta>
- <https://www.wisdomlib.org/definition/vastushanti>
- <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grihapravesha>

### Maha Mrityunjaya homa
- Performed through the Mahamrityunjaya mantra (Rigveda 7.59.12), addressed
  to Rudra/Tryambaka, traditionally for health and long life.
- <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahamrityunjaya_Mantra>

### Navagraha homa / Navagraha Shanti homa
- The nine grahas. *Shanti* denotes the pacificatory form.
- <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Navagraha>

## Yagas and Pujas — NOT researchable, list needed from the priest

There is no canonical list of "the yagas" or "the pujas" the way there is a
canonical list of the sixteen samskaras. Which yagas a priest performs, and
which pujas, is his own inventory — it depends on his training, his lineage,
and what he is actually equipped to conduct. Published sources describe
individual rites but cannot say what this priest offers.

Inventing a plausible list here is exactly the failure mode that produced the
copy this site was cleaned of, so the Yagas and Pujas pages deliberately carry
no named list. **Send the two lists the way the homa list was sent and they
will be added with the same treatment.**

For reference, the distinction the category descriptions rely on: a *homa* is
an offering into a consecrated fire; a *yaga/yajna* is the larger observance,
longer and requiring more priests and preparation. Source:
<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yajna>
